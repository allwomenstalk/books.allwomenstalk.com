# 7 Famous New England Authors and Their Iconic Novels Set in the Region

There are many famous New England authors whose books feature New England, some of them perpetually enchanted by the haunted landscape and others briefly enamored. Some of these classic writers from New England are long gone, while contemporary New England authors are still writing stories that capture both the rich history and the convoluted social structures found across Massachusetts, Maine, New Hampshire, Vermont, Rhode Island, and Connecticut. New England became my home several years ago, though I have long been captivated by its coastal towns, historic forests, and rich literary heritage. If you are looking for novels set in New England or books about New England to add to your reading list, these extraordinary literary figures have shaped how we view the region. Here are my absolute favorite authors from New England and the memorable settings they brought to life.

## 1. Stephen King

![Stephen King](https://resize.allw.mn/1028x0/filters:format(webp)/filters:quality(70)/content/2013/09/05130538_4342_419x619.jpg)

It pretty much goes without saying that when discussing contemporary New England authors, Stephen King stands as one of the most notable and prolific figures in American literature. Visit the [official Stephen King website](https://stephenking.com) to explore his vast bibliography. With only a few exceptions, nearly all of his terrifying books set in New England unfold in Maine, with brief excursions into Connecticut (in _Hearts in Atlantis_, both the collection and short story) and Massachusetts (in _Bag of Bones_, where Jo has family in Lynn and Malden). Born and raised in the region, King's deep connection to his home state shines through every page, even when unleashing cosmic dread and nightmares come true. If you enjoy eerie tales set in atmospheric coastal towns, his work paves the way for other masters of horror like [H. P. Lovecraft's eerie macabre fiction](#6).

## 2. John Irving

![John Irving](https://resize.allw.mn/1028x0/filters:format(webp)/filters:quality(70)/content/2013/09/05130623_2869_399x618.jpg)

John Irving, one of the most celebrated contemporary writers from New England, gives plenty of love to his native New Hampshire, blending true-to-life regional details among fictionalized towns. You'll find distinct New England flavor in most of his acclaimed novels, captured through localized speech, eccentric characters, and vivid campus settings. Irving has a charming tendency to highlight many of his favorite places around the world alongside his New England roots, including Amsterdam.

**Key Themes in New Hampshire & New England Fiction:**

- Small-town secrets and quirky community dynamics
- Historic prep school and college town settings
- Deep family sagas spanning multiple generations

## 3. Wally Lamb

![Wally Lamb](https://resize.allw.mn/1028x0/filters:format(webp)/filters:quality(70)/content/2013/09/05130836_6160_420x685.jpg)

Wally Lamb is another essential voice among authors from New England. Originally from Connecticut with close connections to Rhode Island, his fictional town of Three Rivers serves as a rich backdrop modeled after the mill towns where he lived. Readers looking for authors like Wally Lamb will appreciate his emotional depth and nuanced character studies. You can learn more about his bibliography on [Wally Lamb's official site](https://www.wallylamb.net). He holds the distinction of being one of the few male novelists who writes convincingly from a female perspective; without his name on the cover of _She's Come Undone_, for instance, you would never guess it was written by a man. If you haven't yet read _I Know This Much Is True_ and _The Hour I First Believed_, both stand out as masterclasses in character-driven storytelling, echoing the multi-layered drama seen in [John Irving's classic novels](#2).

## 4. Grace Metalious

![Grace Metalious](https://resize.allw.mn/1028x0/filters:format(webp)/filters:quality(70)/content/2013/09/05131328_1936_314x500.jpg)

Although best known for the scandalous bestseller _Peyton Place_ and its sequel _Return to Peyton Place_, Grace Metalious wrote two other compelling novels set in New Hampshire: _The Tight White Collar_ and _No Adam in Eden_. The _Peyton Place_ books capture the hidden tensions of New Hampshire small towns—a mixture inspired by Alton, Gilmanton, and Laconia. Other novels take place in the real location of Manchester, which is a wonderfully charming and historic town.

## 5. Nathaniel Hawthorne

![Nathaniel Hawthorne](https://resize.allw.mn/1028x0/filters:format(webp)/filters:quality(70)/content/2013/09/05131358_5166_509x654.jpg)

Salem's own Nathaniel Hawthorne had a profound affection for famous Massachusetts authors' history, using Salem in particular as the backdrop for many of his finest novels and short stories. _The Scarlet Letter_ is quite notorious, but _The House of the Seven Gables_—which you can actually tour by visiting [The House of the Seven Gables historic site](https://7gables.org) in Salem—is especially famous. Hawthorne focused heavily on colonial Puritan themes, guilt, and morality, while his later works reflect increasingly apparent transcendentalist themes.

## 6. H. P. Lovecraft

![H. P. Lovecraft](https://resize.allw.mn/1028x0/filters:format(webp)/filters:quality(70)/content/2013/09/05131506_2759_550x770.jpg)

Specializing in cosmic horror, fantasy, science fiction, and the overall strange and macabre, Rhode Island native H. P. Lovecraft harbored a particular love for Massachusetts. You can explore his complete lore on the [H. P. Lovecraft Archive](https://hplovecraft.com). His works are richly textured with thought-provoking themes, especially inhumanity within human nature. Many real places pop up in his works, including Pascoag, Cranston (as Pawtuxet), Newport, and Chepachet in Rhode Island; Boston, nearby Danvers, Bolton, Ipswich, and Salem, Massachusetts; and even Brattleboro, Vermont. Give "Pickman's Model" a read as well, especially if you plan to visit Salem. Based on an entirely true family, and hinting at a delicious family secret that has intrigued readers for generations, you'll find clues about the Pickmans in two of Salem's oldest and most haunted cemeteries. Lovecraft's atmospheric horror builds directly upon the dark historical themes pioneered earlier by [Nathaniel Hawthorne's gothic stories](#5).

## 7. John Updike

![John Updike](https://resize.allw.mn/1028x0/filters:format(webp)/filters:quality(70)/content/2013/09/05131944_5315_600x921.jpg)

John Updike had rather controversial philosophies on a lot of things, but he was a exceptionally gifted writer who deeply appreciated New England settings. Born in Pennsylvania, he eventually settled in Massachusetts and is buried in Danvers, right next door to Peabody. His _Rabbit_ series is excellent, but novels like _The Witches of Eastwick_ and _The Widows of Eastwick_ particularly highlight what life in small town New England is like.

**Must-Visit Literary Landmarks in New England:**

- The House of the Seven Gables (Salem, MA)
- Stephen King's Historic Home & Literary Landmarks (Bangor, ME)
- The Concord Authors' Ridge at Sleepy Hollow Cemetery (Concord, MA)

There are many other writers whose books feature New England, of course, authors of both classic and contemporary literature. The awesome thing is that the entire region is a bookworm's paradise, with the ability to visit all the places featured in your favorite novels. In the case of writers like Hawthorne and Lovecraft, there are even memorials and childhood homes.
