# 7 Reasons Why You Should Take Part in NaNoWriMo This Year

Do you want to take part in NaNoWriMo this year? It's less than two months away, and if you're planning on participating, now is the ideal time to start preparing. For those who haven't come across it before, NaNoWriMo stands for National Novel Writing Month—an annual event that unites writers of all backgrounds from around the globe throughout the month of November. In a nutshell, your goal is to write a first draft of a novel—50,000 words—during those 30 days. That is certainly a major challenge. Even though it means sacrificing a portion of your social calendar for four weeks, it is an extraordinarily rewarding journey to embark on. Whether you are aiming for your first book or building a regular writing routine, encouraging aspiring authors to dive into NaNoWriMo is one of the best things any writer can do!

## 1. Writers Write

This is the most fundamental lesson you will gain from taking part in NaNoWriMo. Out of all the creative advice available for aspiring authors, this principle comes up more than any other: to be a writer, you must actively put words on the page. First drafts are rarely polished or perfect, and they are not supposed to be. NaNoWriMo provides the ultimate incentive to stop overthinking and start writing. If you have had a story idea floating around in your mind and have been hesitating to start the draft, signing up for this challenge will get your momentum going. At the end of November, you will have 50,000 words ready to refine into a complete manuscript.

To help kickstart your momentum each day, consider incorporating a few simple daily writing practices:

- Set a consistent daily word goal (roughly 1,667 words per day).
- Use writing software like [Scrivener](https://www.literatureandlatte.com/scrivener/overview) or simple markdown editors to track progress.
- Write without stopping to edit or check spelling during your writing sprints.

## 2. Practice Makes Perfect

I have never heard of a seasoned author who sat down and produced a flawless manuscript on their very first try. Storytelling and prose are crafts like any other, and the single best way to refine your voice is through consistent practice. Even if only 20,000 of your initial 50,000 words make it into your final draft after editing, that progress is invaluable. Developing speed and building confidence in your storytelling abilities will serve you well into the future, making the challenge entirely worth doing. By focusing on building your writing habit, you lay the foundation for long-term literary success (which you can build on when [overcoming self-doubt](#5) in later stages).

## 3. It's Only Four Weeks

It is true that your social calendar might take a back seat during November when you commit to writing every day. For four weeks, your schedule will likely consist of working, writing during spare hours, and heading to bed—depending on your writing speed and daily pace. However, remember that four weeks is a short period, and careful planning allows you to schedule well-deserved breaks. November is naturally a cozy, quieter month; setting up a dedicated workspace with a warm drink and a focused playlist can transform your evening writing sessions into a peaceful routine.

| NaNoWriMo Benchmark | Daily Word Count | Target Goal |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Week 1 Pace | 1,667 words/day | 11,669 total words |
| Week 2 Pace | 1,667 words/day | 25,000 total words |
| Week 4 Final | 1,667 words/day | 50,000 total words |

Tracking your word counts using a structured benchmark table like the one above ensures you stay on pace throughout the month without feeling overwhelmed on day thirty.

## 4. It's Fun!

If you love storytelling, you likely write because of the joy and satisfaction it brings. While NaNoWriMo demands dedication, it is designed to be an enjoyable and thrilling creative challenge. Combining fun with a genuine sense of personal achievement creates an unbeatable feeling when November ends. Along the way, you also have the opportunity to connect with fellow novelists across the world, creating lasting friendships within the global writing network (especially when engaging with the [vibrant author community](#6) online).

## 5. It's up to You What You Decide to do

Have you ever written a paragraph and immediately felt the urge to delete it? Many writers struggle with an inner critic that slows down their output by constantly editing earlier sentences. NaNoWriMo actively disrupts that habit by prioritizing word volume over immediate perfection. Remember that your draft remains completely private until you choose to share it. Giving yourself permission to write messy first drafts allows your creativity to flow freely, and editing tools like [ProWritingAid](https://prowritingaid.com/) or [Grammarly](https://www.grammarly.com/) can help polish your text once the month is over.

## 6. It's a Community

There are very few events where hundreds of thousands of writers simultaneously work toward the exact same milestone. By signing up on the official [NaNoWriMo website](https://nanowrimo.org/), you gain access to progress trackers, motivational pep talks, local write-ins, and online forums. Reaching the 50,000-word milestone unlocks official winner badges and certificates, offering extra motivation as you navigate the mid-month writing slump (and preparing you for [launching your writing journey](#7)).

## 7. It Could Be Your Debut

Finally, there is the ultimate dream for many participants: the manuscript you draft during November could become your debut novel. Countless published books began as rough NaNoWriMo drafts that were subsequently revised, polished, and submitted to agents. Although the finished manuscript will look vastly different after editing, it only exists because you took the leap and committed to writing those 50,000 words. Giving your story a chance to exist on the page is well worth the effort!

Are you ready to take on the challenge this November? If you decide to participate, there is a wealth of advice and encouragement available from published authors and past participants who have completed the challenge. Start outlining your ideas, organizing your workspace, and gathering your resources before November 1st arrives. Are you planning to join NaNoWriMo this year?
